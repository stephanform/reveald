I’d turn this into a staggered-adoption panel study with two linked parts: a reporting-quality design and a quasi-experimental impact design. The proposal already gives you the core leverage — CSRD rollout is uneven because “implementation was still pending in seven countries, including Germany,” and the regime is being delayed and weakened through “Directive (EU) 2025/794 (‘Stop the Clock’).” That means “CSRD-compliant annual reports are available for a subset of companies, while they are not available for others.”

A clean way to structure it is:

Use the firm-year as the main unit of analysis, with a panel covering a pre-period long enough to establish trends and a post-period beginning when CSRD/ESRS reporting starts to appear in your sample.
Define treatment at the level the regulation actually moves: firm in-scope status × country implementation timing × reporting obligation. That gives you a stronger design than a simple treated-versus-untreated split, because the exposure is staggered and partially incomplete.
Build one primary outcome family for disclosure quality: ESRS coverage, specificity/granularity, double-materiality discussion, use of metrics and targets, and assurance status. That matches the proposal’s own objective of an “up-to-date stocktaking” of NFRD/CSRD/ESRS reporting maturity.
Build a second outcome family for substantive sustainability and performance effects: emissions intensity, energy/resource intensity, target-pathway progress, and a small set of financial/risk outcomes such as ROA, ROE, growth, and volatility. The proposal already frames this as the development of selected sustainability indicators in “temporal, sectoral, and cross-country comparison.”
Make the coding layer explicit and auditable. Start with a pilot of 20–30 reports, lock a codebook, train coders, and record disagreements before the main coding run; the draft already points in this direction with “AI-assisted pilot coding (e.g. 20–30 reports) … and documentation of ambiguities and interpretation cases.”
For identification, use firm and year fixed effects as the baseline, then add a difference-in-differences / event-study framework around implementation timing and exposure. Because treatment arrives at different times and not all firms are treated at once, the event-study piece is important for checking pre-trends and for showing whether reporting quality changes before substantive outcomes do. The proposal’s own modeling list already includes “panel fixed effects, difference-in-differences across implementation timing and exposure, event studies, robustness tests.”
Add robustness layers: alternative treatment definitions, alternative disclosure indices, country-by-year and industry-by-year controls, lagged outcomes, and sensitivity checks for missing data and firm selection into the sample.
If you want the design to be genuinely comprehensive, I would also separate the project into three work packages: first, a descriptive mapping of CSRD/ESRS implementation; second, a disclosure-quality index validated by human coding; third, the causal panel analysis linking regulation to disclosure and, more cautiously, to sustainability and performance outcomes. That keeps the paper honest about what the data can identify cleanly and what it can only associate.
